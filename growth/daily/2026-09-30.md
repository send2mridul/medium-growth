### 7. [The Fallacy of the Stateless Layer: Every System Remembers Something Somewhere](https://medium.com/@johnmwegah/the-fallacy-of-the-stateless-layer-every-system-remembers-something-somewhere-644ffbd5723d)

Ready-to-post comment:

> “Statelessness is an interface property” is the key idea here. A practical consequence is that autoscaling policy should include a downstream state budget. Scaling from 20 to 100 workers is not safe merely because the workers are disposable if it also multiplies database connections, cache-miss traffic, or lease contention by five. Ready-to-post comment:

> The distinction between a board that displays status and a card that triggers movement is powerful. Software teams often visualize queues without defining what anyone must do when a limit is crossed. A useful Kanban policy should turn a visible bottleneck into an explicit action: stop starting work, swarm the blocked item, or escalate a missing skill or dependency. Otherwise the board becomes an accurate picture of delay rather than a mechanism for changing it.
