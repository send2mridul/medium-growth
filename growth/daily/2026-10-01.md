### 2. [Agile Was Built for Human Bandwidth. AI Just Removed the Limit.](https://medium.com/@colebrow.cb/agile-was-built-for-human-bandwidth-ai-just-removed-the-limit-d0a117384e1b)

Coley Brown · 1 day ago · 5 responses

Why engage: The discussion is active but still small, and the author explicitly acknowledges that context switching moved into review. That creates room for a practical counterweight to the parallelism thesis.

Ready-to-post comment:

> “Context switching moved” is the key caveat. Agent capacity may be parallel, but verified-change capacity is still constrained by human attention, integration risk, test environments, and how quickly production signals arrive. I would plan around reviewed and safely absorbed changes rather than agent hours, then cap concurrent agent streams the way Kanban caps WIP. Otherwise faster generation can create a review queue whose age hides more risk than the old sprint queue did.

### 3. [What responsible AI means when agents can build the system](https://medium.com/@viajesubmarino/what-responsible-ai-means-when-agents-can-build-the-system-9aa0336ec708)

Edgar Bermudez · 1 day ago · 3 responses

Why engage: A well-sourced treatment of ownership, recourse, permissions, and human accountability, with an uncrowded discussion and direct overlap with long-lived agents.

Ready-to-post comment:

> The distinction between operational ownership and intellectual-property ownership is especially useful. For long-lived agents, I would add that authorization should decay. A permission approved for one objective, data set, or week should not silently remain valid after the task, policy, or surrounding state changes. Task-scoped credentials, expiry, and a recorded permission version at each consequential action would make the named human owner able to reconstruct not only what the agent did, but why it was allowed to do it then.

### 5. [Your Dashboard Is Current. Its Numbers Aren't.](https://medium.com/@billygareth01/your-dashboard-is-current-its-numbers-arent-f9173e85bcec)

Billy Gareth · Published moments ago

Why engage: A concise data-engineering story with a strong operational premise and no visible discussion yet. Mridul can add a precise data-contract consequence.

Ready-to-post comment:

> Separating provisional from finalized totals is the part many dashboards leave implicit. I would expose two timestamps rather than one: when the view was computed and the event-time watermark through which the data is believed complete. “Updated now” can coexist with “complete through 10:42.” That makes late-data risk visible to the consumer and gives the pipeline a correctness SLO alongside its freshness SLO. Reconciliation gaps then become a first-class metric, not a surprise discovered by Finance.

Shorter alternative:

> “Updated now” needs a companion: “complete through event time X.” Compute time measures pipeline activity; the watermark tells the reader how much of reality the number actually includes.
